**[INTRO — 0:00 to 1:30]**

**[VISUAL: title card with "Week 52 — L4 Capstone — Your Investment Policy Statement"]**

**Stella:** Welcome to week fifty-two. The last week.

**Horace:** Fifty-one weeks ago we started with compound interest
and the case for index funds. Today we put every piece back together
into one document. The investment policy statement.

**Stella:** Horace — what *is* an IPS, in one sentence?

**Horace:** It is the contract between calm-you, who is watching
this video, and panicked-you, who will sometime in the next forty
years stare at a 30% drawdown and want to sell everything. The IPS
is what calm-you writes down to constrain panicked-you.

**Stella:** That is a depressing framing.

**Horace:** It is a realistic framing. If you only do one thing
from this entire course — skip the options, skip the factor tilts,
skip the tail hedges — and you write a one-page IPS and follow it,
you will outperform the median active retail account by a wide
margin. We have the Dalbar evidence from week eleven, the SPIVA
evidence from week forty-three, the deflated-Sharpe evidence from
week forty-six. The integration matters more than the picks.

**Stella:** OK. Let's build the document.

---

**[BLOCK 1 — What an IPS is, six sections — 1:30 to 4:30]**

**[VISUAL: image/week52_full_allocation.png]**

**Stella:** Six sections. Goals. Time horizon. Risk tolerance.
Target allocation. Rebalancing rule. Monitoring KPIs. That's the
whole document.

**Horace:** Plus two appendices nobody else writes down — tax
location and the active-sleeve stop rule. Where each
sleeve sits matters as much as the allocation. And because alpha is
rare, the
default outcome of any active sleeve is that it gets shut down,
so you write the shutdown rule before you start.

**Stella:** Risk tolerance — you're insisting on a number, not a
word.

**Horace:** Yes. "Moderate" is meaningless. "I will not sell into a
drawdown of 35% or less" — that is meaningful. Whatever number you
write down is the line that keeps you honest. If your portfolio
hits 80% of that drawdown, you call a review meeting with yourself.
If it hits 100%, your IPS already specified what to do.

**Stella:** What if I write 35% and then live through 2008 at -55%?

**Horace:** Then your IPS was wrong, and you rewrite it on a calm
day after the market recovers. The IPS is a living document. But
you do not rewrite it *during* the drawdown — that is when the IPS
is at its most valuable.

---

**[BLOCK 2 — The seven-sleeve model — 4:30 to 9:00]**

**[VISUAL: image/week52_full_allocation.png — pause on the pie]**

**Horace:** The seven sleeves of the L4 model. 35% global equity,
25% income, 15% gold, 5% Bitcoin, 10% CTA, 5% tail hedge, 5% cash.

**Stella:** Map that to the four tranches.

**Horace:** Map to four tranches. **Growth tranche** is the 35% global equity —
broad VTI plus factor tilts AVUV and MTUM. **Income tranche** is
the 25% — bond ladder, dividend equity, premium-write overlay
from week 36. **Store-of-value tranche** is gold + Bitcoin, 20%
total — gold for the established belief asset and Bitcoin as the
right-tail barbell. **Opportunistic tranche** is CTA and tail
hedge, 15% total. Plus 5% cash.

**Stella:** The 5% Bitcoin slice is going to upset half our
audience.

**Horace:** Both halves. The "crypto is a Ponzi" half thinks 5% is
too high. The "crypto is the future" half thinks 5% is too low.
The 5% number sits between them on purpose. At 5%, a 90% drawdown
in the Bitcoin sleeve costs the portfolio 4.5%. That is survivable.
At 5%, a 10x in the Bitcoin sleeve adds 45% to the portfolio. That
is meaningful. Right-tail barbell — small enough to lose,
large enough to matter.

**Stella:** And the tail hedge — 5% on long-dated SPY puts.

**Horace:** Universa-style sleeve from week 47. Bleeds 1-2% per
year in calm markets. Pays 30-60% of NAV in a -30% drawdown. The
asymmetry is the point. And — this is critical — the cash sleeve
right next to it is what *funds* the premium roll. The two sleeves
are paired.

**[BLOCK 3 — The backtest — 9:00 to 13:30]**

**[VISUAL: image/week52_backtest_compare.png]**

**Stella:** Sixteen years of data, four portfolios. Walk us through
the numbers.

**Horace:** S&P 500 wins on raw return — 13.1% CAGR. Of course it
does. It also has its worst calendar year at -18% — and the
intra-year peak-to-trough was -25% to -34% in 2020 and 2022. The
L4 model earns 8.3% — five points worse than the S&P — with a
worst calendar year of -2.7% and a vol of 6.7%. Half the realised
vol, two-thirds the return, and a Sharpe of 0.94 against the S&P's
0.84. That trade is the case for the diversifier sleeves.

**Stella:** And against 60/40?

**Horace:** Same Sharpe story. 60/40 ends with slightly more
terminal wealth over this exact window because it has 60% equity
and the equity market did spectacularly well. The L4 has 35%
equity. So in a 16-year run that started in 2010 and is therefore
overweight a generational bull market, 60/40 wins on raw final
wealth. But the L4 has 6.7% vol versus 60/40's 9.1%, and a worst
year of -2.7% versus -16%. *Risk-adjusted*, the L4 wins. The
reason is 2020 and 2022 — in every other year over the window,
the L4 lagged plain 60/40 by tiny amounts because the CTA and
tail-hedge sleeves were bleeding. Then 2020 happened, then 2022
happened, and the bleeding sleeves printed enough crisis-alpha to
close the vol and Sharpe gap and crush the worst-year number.

**Stella:** Vol-tail-wags-dog, exactly.

**Horace:** The two crisis years are wagging the dog. That is the
case for the diversifier sleeves in one chart. Without the
diversifiers, 2022 hands you the same -16% calendar-year hit as
the 60/40. With them — the L4 line shows it — you get through
2022 with a flat-to-slightly-positive year.

**Stella:** Big disclaimer though.

**Horace:** Enormous. Week 46 stacked every disclaimer on
backtests. Survivorship, look-ahead, regime-dependence. The
backtest does not prove the L4 model works going forward. It
suggests the *shape* is sensible. The next twenty years will
contain regimes the last sixteen did not. Do not quote the
historical Sharpe number to anyone, including yourself, as a
forecast.

---

**[BLOCK 4 — Rebalancing and tax location — 13:30 to 17:00]**

**Stella:** Rebalancing rule.

**Horace:** Hybrid. Annual review on a calendar date — first
business day of January is fine. Plus a band trigger — any sleeve
drifting more than five percentage points from target gets a
mid-year trim or add. That's the default I recommend. Calendar
keeps it simple. Band keeps it honest.

**Stella:** And in a taxable account?

**Horace:** New contributions go to whatever's underweight first.
Sells happen only when the band genuinely demands it, and you
look for tax-loss harvesting opportunities at the same time. In
the IRA you rebalance freely — no friction.

**Stella:** Tax location.

**Horace:** Tax-location capstone. Bonds in IRA — coupon income is
ordinary, sheltering it dominates. Qualified-dividend equity in
taxable — the LTCG schedule is too good to waste. Gold and
Bitcoin in Roth IRA if available — gold dodges the 28% collectibles
rate, Bitcoin captures the asymmetric upside tax-free. The map is
in the appendix. Most portfolios that look identical on the
allocation level differ by 0.5 to 1.0% per year purely on where
each sleeve sits.

**Stella:** Over thirty years, that is.

**Horace:** Fifteen to thirty percent of terminal wealth. The map
matters.

---

**[BLOCK 5 — Monitoring KPIs — 17:00 to 19:00]**

**Stella:** Four numbers, quarterly.

**Horace:** Rolling 12-month Sharpe. Realised vol. Trailing peak
drawdown. Sleeve drift. Spreadsheet. Quarterly. Done.

**Stella:** And not daily.

**Horace:** Daily checking *generates* the Dalbar gap from week
eleven. The most successful investors are dead investors and
forgetters. Quarterly is the closest a living person gets to
forgetfulness without actively trying.

**Stella:** What do you do with the numbers?

**Horace:** Mostly nothing. The IPS already says what the rules
are. The KPIs exist so that if the rules are being violated by
reality — vol systematically higher than spec, drawdown bigger
than tolerance — you catch it before it becomes a crisis. Most
quarters you write the four numbers in the cell, see that nothing
has crossed a threshold, and close the spreadsheet.

---

**[BLOCK 6 — The IPS builder interactive — 19:00 to 21:00]**

**[VISUAL: course/interactive/week52_ips_builder.html]**

**Stella:** The interactive.

**Horace:** Four inputs. Your age. Your retirement age. Your
current portfolio size. Your target retirement income. The tool
emits a draft IPS — the seven-sleeve allocation tilted by your
horizon, the rebalancing rule, the monitoring cadence, and a
projected wealth path with median plus 5th and 95th percentile
bands.

**Stella:** And the projected wealth path uses what assumptions?

**Horace:** The historical mean and vol of the L4 model from the
backtest, with a Monte Carlo over the remaining horizon. The
median is the central estimate. The 5th and 95th percentiles are
the realistic best- and worst-case bands. The width of the band is
itself a teaching tool — the further out you project, the wider
it gets, and that width is what humility about forecasting actually
looks like.

**Stella:** Last thing. Stop rule for active sleeves.

**Horace:** Five percent per year underperformance versus benchmark
over a rolling five-year window cuts the sleeve to half weight.
The number is illustrative. The commitment to write a number — any
number — *before* the underperformance starts is the discipline.
Alpha is rare. The default outcome is that any active
sleeve gets shut down. The IPS specifies how.
