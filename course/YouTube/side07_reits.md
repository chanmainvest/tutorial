**VIDEO TITLE:** REITs — Real Estate Without the Real Estate | Side Lesson 7

**RUNTIME TARGET:** ~11 minutes

**HOSTS:**
- **Horace** (teacher): Career investor, has watched the public-REIT
  wrapper detach from physical real estate three times since 2000.
- **Stella** (student): Retail investor with a 401(k) target-date fund
  and a question about whether to add VNQ on top.

---

**[INTRO SEQUENCE]**

[VISUAL: Animated logo "Side Lesson 7 — REITs"]

[VISUAL: image/side07_reit_sectors.png — VNQ sector composition.]

**Horace:** *(holding up a photo of a shopping mall and a photo of a
data centre)* Stella, quick test. If I bought VNQ — Vanguard's REIT
ETF — in 2010, which of these did I mostly own?

**Stella:** Mall, probably?

**Horace:** Right. In 2010 you did. Today, that same ETF is mostly
*this one* — data centres, cell towers, industrial logistics. About
forty per cent of the modern VNQ is digital infrastructure. The mall
weight is a single-digit number now. The mental model most retail
investors have for "REITs" is fifteen years out of date.

**Stella:** OK so they're not malls. What are they then?

**Horace:** Stocks. That's the punchline of the whole lesson. REITs
are stocks that happen to own buildings. They trade on the equity
exchanges, they panic when the equity market panics, and they move on
interest rates with about a seven-year duration. They are not real
estate in the way most people use the phrase.

---

**[SEGMENT 1: THE WRAPPER — 90 PERCENT DISTRIBUTE, NO CORPORATE TAX]**

**Horace:** Let me give you the structure in thirty seconds. A REIT is
a corporate entity that elects to be taxed under sections eight-five-
six through eight-five-nine. Four tests. Seventy-five per cent of
assets in real estate. Seventy-five per cent of income from rents or
mortgage interest. Ninety per cent of taxable income distributed every
year. And at least a hundred shareholders.

**Stella:** And in return?

**Horace:** No corporate income tax. The trade-off is that you, the
investor, receive distributions taxed at *ordinary income* rates, not
the qualified-dividend rate. Section 199A from the 2017 tax bill —
made permanent in 2025 — gives you a twenty-per-cent deduction on
those distributions. So if you're in the thirty-seven per cent
bracket, the effective top federal rate on REIT income is twenty-nine
point six per cent. Higher than qualified dividends at twenty per
cent. Lower than full ordinary at thirty-seven.

**Stella:** So tax-wise they're worse than SCHD.

**Horace:** Yes. Which is why location-not-allocation matters here.
REITs go in the IRA first. Taxable account only when
you've explicitly priced the after-tax yield against alternatives.

---

**[SEGMENT 2: SECTOR COMPOSITION — DATA CENTRES, TOWERS, LOGISTICS]**

[VISUAL: image/side07_reit_sectors.png — VNQ sector pie.]

**Horace:** Here is what's actually inside VNQ as of April 2026. Cell
towers — fourteen per cent. Data centres — fifteen per cent.
Industrial logistics — ten per cent. Residential — nine per cent.
Healthcare — eight per cent. Self-storage — six per cent. Retail —
four per cent. Office — five per cent. Hotels and specialty — the
rest.

**Stella:** That looks like infrastructure, not real estate.

**Horace:** Right. American Tower, Crown Castle, SBA Communications —
those three companies are the vast majority of US cell tower
ownership, and they're REITs because the FCC and the IRS agreed in
2012 that towers are real property. Equinix and Digital Realty
similarly converted. So the modern public-REIT universe is heavily
digital infrastructure with a side of warehouse roofs. If you wanted a
mall index, you'd buy SPG specifically — and you would have lost
money on it for a decade.

**Stella:** So the sector ETFs — SRVR for towers, VPN for data —
that's how you'd over-weight the modern theme.

**Horace:** That's the toolkit. Most investors should default to plain
VNQ and skip the overlay. If you want a single-sector tilt, five to
ten per cent on top of a core VNQ position is the right size, not a
hundred-per-cent substitution.

---

**[SEGMENT 3: RATE SENSITIVITY — THE 2022 EXAMPLE]**

[VISUAL: image/side07_reit_vs_stocks.png — VNQ vs SPY vs IEF wealth
path 2000-Apr2026.]

**Horace:** This chart is the most important slide in this lesson.
Three lines. Blue is SPY — total return on the S&P 500. Green is IEF —
the seven-to-ten year Treasury ETF. Gold is VNQ. From January 2000
through April 2026, all three normalised to a dollar.

**Stella:** VNQ tracks SPY pretty closely most of the time.

**Horace:** And then it doesn't. Look at 2008 — VNQ falls thirty-eight
per cent, deeper than SPY. Look at 2020 March — VNQ falls twenty-five
per cent in three weeks. Look at 2022 — and this is the one — SPY
loses eighteen per cent on rates. VNQ loses *twenty-five* per cent.
IEF loses about fifteen per cent. VNQ takes the worst of both worlds:
the equity drawdown when stocks fall, the duration drawdown when
rates rise.

**Stella:** Why?

**Horace:** Because a REIT is a long sequence of distributions, and
the present value of a long sequence of distributions moves inversely
with the discount rate. The empirical duration of VNQ is six to eight.
Same as a seven-year Treasury for rate-shock purposes. When the ten-
year yield rose two-fifty basis points in 2022, the duration math
predicted a twenty-five-per-cent drop. That's exactly what happened.

**Stella:** And meanwhile, private real estate?

**Horace:** Roughly flat that year. Cap rates moved in the
transactions market but not nearly enough to justify minus twenty-
five per cent. The buildings did not lose a quarter of their value.
The wrapper did. The framing is vol-tail-wags-the-dog — when you put
a public quote on a long-duration cash flow, the public quote will
move on rates whether or not the underlying does.

---

**[SEGMENT 4: SIZING AND PLACEMENT]**

**Horace:** Last segment. Where does this thing actually go?

**Stella:** Income sleeve, IRA, five to ten per cent.

**Horace:** Exactly that. Tranche-three placement — income sleeve, not
the stores-of-value sleeve, not the growth sleeve. Account placement —
IRA before taxable, every time you have the room. Size — five to ten
per cent of the equity portion of the portfolio, capped. If you find
yourself wanting to size REITs above fifteen per cent, you don't want
public REITs. You want a rental property with a thirty-year fixed
mortgage. That's a different asset class with a different vol profile,
and we cover the trade-off in side fourteen on private markets.

**Stella:** And the misconception we should leave the audience with?

**Horace:** Public REITs let you own real estate the way an ETF lets
you own stocks. The ETF inherits the volatility of the wrapper, not
the patience of the underlying. You get equity-market vol, you get
duration risk, and you give up the qualified-dividend tax break. In
return you get diversified, liquid, professional management of a
hundred-and-sixty buildings for thirteen basis points. That's the
trade. Worth taking, in size, in the right account. Not worth
mistaking for owning a building.
