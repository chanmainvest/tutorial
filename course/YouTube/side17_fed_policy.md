---

**[SECTION 1 — The Dual Mandate]**

**[VISUAL: image/side17_ffr_history.png]**

HORACE: First, what is the Fed targeting? Two things: maximum employment
and stable prices. Stable prices is operationalised as 2% core PCE — the
target since the 2012 Bernanke statement. Maximum employment has no
number, but the Fed estimates the non-accelerating inflation rate of
unemployment, NAIRU, currently around 4%, and tries not to push below it
without inflation responding.

When the targets agree, easy meeting. When they disagree, the Fed has to
choose. In 2008 it chose employment over prices and slashed to zero
while CPI was still 5%. In 2022 it chose prices over employment and
hiked into a labour market still adding 400k jobs a month. That choice
*is* the Fed's regime stance.

STELLA: So I should treat the dual mandate as a regime indicator?

HORACE: Exactly. Inflation-fighting Fed: flat curve, real rates up,
long-duration assets compress. Employment-fighting Fed: steep curve,
real rates down, duration gets bid. Side 06 covered the inflation
data; this lesson covers the lever.

---

**[SECTION 2 — The FOMC and the Dot Plot]**

**[VISUAL: image/side17_dot_plot.png]**

HORACE: The Federal Open Market Committee is twelve voting members:
seven Board governors, the New York Fed president — permanent — and
four of the eleven other regional presidents on a one-year rotation.
They meet eight times a year. Four of those meetings — March, June,
September, December — come with the Summary of Economic Projections.

Inside the SEP is the dot plot. Sixteen or seventeen dots, one per
participant, voting or non-voting. Each dot says where that person
thinks the appropriate Fed funds rate will be at year-end for the next
three years and in the longer run.

STELLA: Sixteen dots, twelve voters — why the mismatch?

HORACE: Everyone projects, only twelve vote. Read the median, the
central tendency — drop the top three and bottom three — and the
dispersion. A tight cluster is consensus. A wide spread is committee
disagreement and therefore optionality.

Two reading rules. The median moves markets, the tails don't. And the
longer-run dot is the implicit estimate of "neutral." In April 2026
that longer-run dot sits at 2.75-3.00%. That is up from 2.5% for most
of the 2010s. That single 25-bp shift is the single most important
number in this entire video. It is the regime change this lesson has been
tracking.

---

**[SECTION 3 — The Toolkit]**

HORACE: Pre-2008 the Fed had one tool: it traded Treasury repo to keep
the overnight rate near a single target. Now it has more.

The Fed funds *target range* — since December 2008 the FOMC announces
a 25-bp range, not a point. Today, 3.50 to 3.75%. The effective rate
floats inside that range, governed by two administered rates.

IORB, interest on reserve balances — what the Fed pays banks on
reserves. Soft floor. 3.65% today.

ON-RRP, overnight reverse repo — what the Fed pays money-market funds
and GSEs to park cash overnight. Hard floor, 5 bp below IORB. The ON-RRP
ballooned from zero to 2.4 trillion at the 2022 peak. As of April 2026
it's back to about 200 billion. That collapse is a major signal of
liquidity normalisation.

The discount window — lender of last resort, 25 bp above the upper
bound. Used by stigmatised banks in crisis.

Then the balance sheet. When rates hit zero in 2008 and 2020, the Fed
bought Treasuries and mortgage-backed securities outright to push down
long-end yields. Peak balance sheet: 8.97 trillion in August 2022. Now
6.5 trillion and falling. Each trillion of runoff is roughly 25 bp of
conventional tightening per Fed staff estimates.

STELLA: So I need to read the dot plot and the balance-sheet plan
together?

HORACE: Always.

---

**[SECTION 4 — Transmission]**

HORACE: The Fed sets one overnight rate. Asset prices live on the long
end. The path from one to the other is the transmission mechanism.
Five steps.

One: FFR to SOFR — secured overnight. Pass-through near one-for-one,
SOFR sits 5-10 bp below the upper bound.

Two: SOFR to the 2-year Treasury. Pass-through about 0.85.

Three: 2-year to 10-year. The slope. Pass-through to the long end is
only about 0.5 — term premium and growth expectations soak up the rest.

Four: 10-year to 30-year mortgage. Primary-secondary spread averages
175 bp; in stress it widens to 250-300 bp like March 2020 and October
2022.

Five: 10-year to equity P/E via the Gordon model. Hold the equity risk
premium at 5%, real growth at 2%; a 100-bp move in the 10y moves fair
P/E by about four multiple points.

**[VISUAL: interactive/side17_fed_lab.html]**

The interactive Fed Lab on the page lets you put in your own end-2026
and end-2027 Fed funds and runoff pace, and the same pass-through
coefficients spit out the implied 2y, 10y, 30y mortgage, and S&P 500
P/E. Play with it. Match your numbers against the median dot. If
they're more than 50 bp apart, you're taking a forecasting bet.

---

**[SECTION 5 — Three Regimes]**

HORACE: Twenty-five years, three regimes.

2000-2007: conventional. Greenspan, Bernanke. FFR swings from 6.5 to 1
to 5.25. Balance sheet flat at 870 billion. Textbook world.

2008-2021: zero lower bound. Lehman, December 2008 cut to zero. Held
there until December 2015. First normalisation peaked at 2.5% in
December 2018. The 2019 mid-cycle adjustment cut three times. Then
COVID, March 2020: emergency to zero in two weeks, two and a half
trillion of QE in three months. Held at zero through March 2022.

Thirteen of those fourteen years had Fed funds below 1%. Anyone who
started trading in 2010 had no first-hand experience of a positive
real funds rate until 2023. That entire cohort had the wrong
calibration.

2022-2026: rapid hiking and normalisation. 525 bps in eighteen months —
fastest since Volcker. Terminal 5.50% in July 2023. First cut September
2024. Today, 3.625%. Dot plot pointing to 3.0% by end-2027 and a
longer-run neutral near 2.75-3.00%.

STELLA: What's the lesson?

HORACE: The forty-year bond bull ended on your watch. Build your
barbell on the assumption that the Fed put strikes 20% lower
than it used to, not 5%. The 2008-2021 muscle memory of "Fed always
rescues duration on a 10% drawdown" is wrong for the next decade.

Three: do not size up around FOMC week. Sell the implied vol if you can
take the risk; sit out if you can't. The vol tail wags the dog.
