## Part 2: YouTube Script

---

**VIDEO TITLE:** Building Your First Real Portfolio — The L1 Capstone | Week 12

**RUNTIME TARGET:** ~18 minutes

**HOSTS:** Horace, Stella

---

**[INTRO — 0:00]**

**Horace:** Eleven weeks. We've covered why we invest, what an index
fund is, what risk really means, why 60/40 worked for forty years,
why diversification is correlation arithmetic, why fees compound
against you, and why your own behaviour is the largest unforced
error on the table. This week, we *build the thing*.

**Stella:** This is the lesson where a viewer who's been following
along should actually open a brokerage account, place trades, and
walk away with a real portfolio.

**Horace:** Right. Not a plan. A portfolio. By Friday.

**Stella:** What's the one rule of this lesson if I had to summarise?

**Horace:** Get the *first month* right. Most retail mistakes happen
in the first month, not the tenth year. After month one, you mostly
just need to leave it alone.

---

**[SEGMENT 1: THE SIX-STEP BUILD — 1:00]**

**Horace:** Six steps, in order. Goal and horizon. Risk tolerance.
Account type. Allocation. Instrument. Implementation.

**Stella:** Where do most people stall?

**Horace:** Step one. Goal and horizon forces you to decide what your
life looks like in twenty years. The portfolio is downstream of that.
We can't make it for you.

**Stella:** And once you have the goal?

**Horace:** The horizon sets the equity ceiling. Money you need in
three years is not in stocks. Money you need in thirty years is
mostly in stocks. That's the rule that drives everything below it.

---

**[SEGMENT 2: ACCOUNT TYPE — 2:00]**

**Horace:** Account type matters more than any single ETF you'll
choose. Wrong account, right ETFs — you leave double-digit basis
points on the table every year for decades.

**Stella:** The hierarchy?

**Horace:** 401(k) up to the employer match first. That match is a
50–100% instant return on the matched dollars. Nothing else in this
lesson comes close.

**Stella:** Then?

**Horace:** HSA if you have a high-deductible health plan. Triple
tax-advantaged — deductible going in, growth tax-free, withdrawals
for medical tax-free. It's a stealth retirement account.

**Stella:** Then Roth IRA?

**Horace:** Then Roth IRA. Tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals
after 59½, no required distributions. Cleanest long-horizon
compounding vehicle in the US tax code.

**Stella:** And taxable?

**Horace:** Last. The flexible bucket. Any amount, any time, but
every dividend and every realised gain is taxed in the year it
happens. The largest unspoken fee on a successful retail
book is *capital gains tax*.

---

**[SEGMENT 3: THE FIVE ARCHETYPES — 5:00]**

**Horace:** [VISUAL: image/week12_archetype_allocations.png] Five
starter portfolios on screen. Early career, mid career, late career,
retired, and the FIRE / barbell-curious row.

**Stella:** Walk me through them.

**Horace:** Early career — 20s and early 30s — is 85% equity. 65 in
US, 20 in ex-US. 5 in bonds, 0 in TIPS, 5 in gold, 5 in cash. Long
horizon, recovery time ample. Aggressive but not 100/0.

**Stella:** Why not 100/0 if you're young?

**Horace:** Because the investor who panic-sells a 100/0 portfolio
in a -40% year ends up with a worse realised return than the
investor who holds 85/10/5 through it. The 5% gold and 5% cash is
behavioural insurance.

**Stella:** Mid career?

**Horace:** Mid 30s and 40s. 65% equity, split 50/15 US and ex-US.
20% intermediate bonds, 5% TIPS, 5% gold, 5% cash. The textbook 60/40
with a gold and TIPS sleeve added.

**Stella:** Late career?

**Horace:** Conservative. 45% equity, 40% bonds plus TIPS, 10% gold,
5% cash. Sequence-of-returns risk dominates here — a -40% drawdown
five years before retirement is the worst thing that can happen.

**Stella:** Retired?

**Horace:** 30% equity, 50% bonds plus TIPS, 10% gold, 10% cash.
Protect the floor, accept smaller upside.

**Stella:** And the FIRE row?

**Horace:** [PAUSE] This one is different. 50% equity, 15% bonds
plus TIPS, 20% gold, 15% cash. Cash-heavy, gold-heavy, equity-
moderate. The structurally-mediocre middle is partly removed.

**Stella:** That's almost the full barbell shape.

**Horace:** It's the *bridge* to the barbell. The full barbell
shape is L2 material. This row is the closest L1 lets you get
without leaving the cap-weighted index core.

---

**[SEGMENT 4: THE ETFS — 9:00]**

**Horace:** Four ETFs is a complete portfolio. VTI for US stocks.
VXUS for ex-US. BND for bonds. GLD or IAU for gold. Three ETFs if
you skip gold — VTI plus VXUS plus BND.

**Stella:** Expense ratios?

**Horace:** VTI is three basis points. VXUS is seven. BND is three.
GLD is forty, IAU is twenty-five. The total weighted expense on a
four-fund moderate portfolio is well under ten basis points.

**Stella:** What about the alternatives — VOO, SPY, AGG?

**Horace:** Within a few basis points of each other and 99%
correlated. Pick one, keep it for life. The choice doesn't matter
nearly as much as the holding.

**Stella:** SCHD? BNDW? TIP?

**Horace:** SCHD is a US dividend tilt — useful if you want a
slightly value-flavoured equity sleeve in a taxable account where
qualified dividends are tax-efficient. BNDW is global aggregate
bonds if you want a small ex-US bond sleeve. SCHP or VTIP for the
TIPS line. All optional. The four-fund standard is enough.

---

**[SEGMENT 5: THE FIRST DEPOSIT — 11:30]**

**Horace:** You have $50,000 to deploy. Lump sum tomorrow, or
spread it across the next 12 months?

**Stella:** Academic answer?

**Horace:** Lump sum wins about two-thirds of the time. Markets go
up most of the time; being out costs you opportunity.

**Stella:** Practical answer?

**Horace:** Dollar-cost average across six months. Twenty-five
percent on day one to get the account open and the trades in. Then
12.5% per month for the next six months.

**Stella:** Why not the academic answer?

**Horace:** Because *behaviourally robust* matters more than
*expected-value optimal* for the first portfolio. The DCA plan
guarantees you do not put 100% of your starting cash into the
highest day of the year. It guarantees that the first time the
market falls 5%, your next purchase is at the lower price. That
trains the reflex you need to survive the rest of the lesson plan.

**Stella:** What's the cost of the DCA over the lump-sum?

**Horace:** Fifty to a hundred basis points on the deployed amount
over the first year, on average. Small price for the behavioural
insurance.

---

**[SEGMENT 6: MONITORING CADENCE — 13:30]**

**Horace:** Quarterly is plenty. Five minutes per quarter to glance
at the account and confirm contributions are flowing.

**Stella:** And the rebalance?

**Horace:** Annual. Bring every sleeve back to target, by selling
the over-weights and buying the under-weights — or by steering the
next quarter of contributions, which is the tax-efficient way.

**Stella:** Anything else?

**Horace:** Life events. Re-review the allocation when something
material changes — new job, marriage, child, large windfall,
retirement date moves. Not on a market move. On a *life* move.

---

**[SEGMENT 7: THE ARCHETYPE OUTCOMES — 15:00]**

**Horace:** [VISUAL: image/week12_archetype_outcomes.png] Five
lines, one for each archetype, $1 invested in 1928, real terms,
log scale.

**Stella:** Order from highest to lowest terminal wealth?

**Horace:** Early career, mid career, FIRE, late career, retired.

**Stella:** Why does FIRE beat late career? FIRE has more cash.

**Horace:** Because FIRE has more *gold* — and gold worked
spectacularly across the 1970s and the 2000s, exactly the windows
where the bond-heavy late-career and retired rows lost real value.
Gold's belief earns its keep in the inflation regimes.

**Stella:** And the early-career line?

**Horace:** Steepest, but also the deepest drawdowns. Over 50% in
1932. Almost 50% in 2008. The line climbs the highest because over
a century equities compound the fastest. The troughs are the price
of admission.

**Stella:** Pick the line whose troughs you can sleep through.

**Horace:** Exactly that.
